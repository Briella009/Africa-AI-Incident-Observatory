# AAIO/AJIM frozen analysis results

## Analysis frame

These results are descriptive statistics from the frozen AAIO research inputs. The core incident table is fixed at 19 records; the multilingual discovery layer contains 9 source-led candidates and remains analytically separate from the core. The watchlist contains 3 excluded records. Counts describe documented evidence in the frozen corpus, not incident prevalence in African countries.

## Geographic concentration and gaps

The 19 core records cover 9 primary countries. Nigeria and South Africa contribute 5 records each (26.3% each), and Kenya contributes 3 (15.8%). Together, these three countries account for 13 of 19 core records (68.4%). West Africa contains 7 core records, Southern Africa 6, East Africa 5, and the combined North/East Africa label 1.

The multilingual discovery layer contains 9 candidates across 7 countries. Six countries were absent from the frozen core: Algeria, Angola, Egypt, Morocco, Niger and Senegal. Eight of the 9 multilingual candidates (88.9%) are from countries not represented in the core. If the two layers are considered only as a discovery pool, unique primary-country coverage rises from 9 to 15, an increase of 6 countries or 66.7%. This is a documentation-coverage effect, not a prevalence estimate.

## Language effect

The discovery extension was intentionally balanced at 3 records each in French, Arabic and Portuguese. French yielded 3 countries, of which 2 were new relative to the core. Arabic yielded 3 countries and all 3 were new relative to the core. Portuguese yielded 3 records from 1 country, Angola, which was new relative to the core.

Translation uncertainty was low for 6 of 9 records (66.7%) and medium for 3 of 9 (33.3%); no record was coded high. AI attribution was coded confirmed for 8 of 9 candidates (88.9%) and probable for 1 of 9 (11.1%). All 9 records remain candidates rather than automatically entering the core.

## Evidence confidence

Core evidence confidence is A for 10 of 19 records (52.6%), B for 8 (42.1%) and C for 1 (5.3%). The multilingual candidate layer contains 7 A records (77.8%) and 2 B records (22.2%), with no C or D records.

The difference should not be interpreted as proof that non-English evidence is stronger. The multilingual set is a small, purposively selected source-led sample built from established fact-checking organisations, so its confidence distribution reflects the collection design.

## Source patterns

Seventeen of 19 frozen core records (89.5%) have a populated AIID cross-reference and use a curated incident database as the primary source type. The two core records without a populated AIID identifier use peer-reviewed research and a primary judicial record respectively. This demonstrates a strong discovery and provenance dependence on existing global incident infrastructure in the frozen core.

Secondary-source labels are more heterogeneous: 12 distinct source-type labels occur across 19 records. The most common is independent fact-check via AIID record at 6 of 19 (31.6%).

The multilingual layer is concentrated in four source organisations: AFP Factuel 3 records, Misbar 3, Verifica.ao 2 and Lusa Verifica 1. The two largest organisations therefore account for 6 of 9 candidates (66.7%). The language expansion improves geographic visibility but does not yet constitute a diversified or representative source network.

## Taxonomy and interoperability loss

Across the 29 OECD common reporting-framework criteria, AAIO maps 3 directly (10.3%), 6 partially (20.7%), 3 approximately (10.3%) and leaves 17 unmapped (58.6%). Only 3 of the 7 mandatory criteria are directly transferable without semantic inference (42.9%); 1 is partial, 1 approximate and 2 unmapped.

Because mandatory criteria 3 (how the AI system relates to the incident) and 10 (severity) are globally unmapped in the frozen AAIO-to-OECD mapping, zero of the 19 core records can satisfy a strict all-mandatory-direct criterion under the current schema. This is a schema-coverage finding, not an OECD compliance judgment.

For the 9 AIID core concepts represented in AAIO's interoperability map, 2 are direct, 2 partial, 1 approximate, 3 unmapped and 1 is cross-reference-only. The largest recurring semantic losses are developer/deployer entity roles, taxonomy annotations and external severity semantics.

## Conservative exclusion signal

The watchlist contains 3 records excluded from the core because the reviewed evidence did not establish the required AI linkage or causation. This supports the methodological claim that the project separates consequential automation or biometric failures from AI incidents when AI involvement is not sufficiently established.

## Result boundary

No result in this package supports ranking countries by AI safety, estimating national incident rates, or claiming representative continental coverage. The principal empirical result is that targeted multilingual discovery changed what became visible in the documented evidence pool, while interoperability analysis showed substantial semantic loss when local evidence fields were aligned to a global reporting framework.
